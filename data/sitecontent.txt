ABOUT THE GROUP
Psychotherapy Supervision Group in Delhi offers professional peer supervision for counsellors and psychotherapists. 
The group provides a reflective learning space focused on deepening clinical understanding and ethical practice.

SUPERVISION APPROACH
We follow relational, psychodynamic, and integrative supervision models. 
Group discussions explore transference, countertransference, and therapist self-awareness.

MEETINGS
Small-group supervision meetings are held monthly. Participants share clinical material in a confidential setting. 
New members are welcome to inquire about upcoming groups.

An experiential account of the journey of psychotherapy training in India

Learning psychotherapy under supervision is an exciting but painful process for the novice in the field. Psychotherapy training in India mostly has been perceived within the boundaries of culture and religion. The psychotherapy training in India takes place in institutional context, as part of the academic degree. The present paper provides an experiential account of psychotherapy training at NIMHANS Bangalore India. The account has been divided in different sections relevant to psychotherapy training. The retrospective account shows similarities in the journey of learning psychotherapy of Indian trainees as reported in psychotherapy training literature. It shows that therapist development in India follows shared philosophy of different models of therapist development. The paper concludes that at ground level, the „psychotherapy training in India‟ goes beyond discussions on cultural and religious issues and that perception about „psychotherapy in India‟ need not remain confined only to cultural and religious heritage.

HIstory of psychology training in India
For psychology post-graduates, the psychotherapy training is embedded in clinical psychology degree, which is a two years intensive course. The formal clinical psychology training started in 1951 at Banaras Hindu University (BHU), Varanasi, as a one year diploma course. But the real inception of clinical psychology training was initiated at National Institute of Mental Health And Neuro Sciences (NIMHANS). It is a multidisciplinary Institute for patient care and academic pursuit in the frontier area of Mental Health and Neuro Sciences. The Lunatic Asylum which came into being in the latter part of the 19th Century was renamed as Mental Hospital in 1925 by the erstwhile Government of Mysore. This hospital and All India Institute of Mental Health established in 1954 by Government of India were amalgamated on 27th December 1974, and thus was formed the autonomous National Institute of Mental Health and Neuro Sciences (NIMHANS). On November 14, 1994, NIMHANS was declared a Deemed University by the University Grants Commission, with academic autonomy. A unique model of a successful partnership between the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India and the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of Karnataka, NIMHANS has been declared an Institute of National Importance by an Act of Parliament (38/2012).
The department of clinical psychology started first clinical psychology training  program  in  1955,  a  two-year  course  titled  ―Diploma  in  Medical Psychology‖. Since then, the department has undertaken periodic revisions in its training program that reflect the expanding horizons in the application of psychology to the field of mental health. The department currently offers a two year full time M. Phil program in Clinical Psychology. A three-year doctoral program is being offered from the year 1967. Extensive direct engagement with the clients and close one-to-one supervision are integral components of both the training programs.
Since inception NIMHANS remains the ultimate choice for training in psychotherapy skills. It provides a dynamic environment for training unmatched by any other institution in India.
